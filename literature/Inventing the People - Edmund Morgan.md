#### Key Points
- We in the West tend to invent "fictions" that form the basis of government (read: authority) by the few over the many. 
- These fictions, however, tend to take on a life of their own that constrain the governors sometimes as much, or more, as the governed. 
	- Put another way, these fictions create "games", the rules of which may be exploited by subject as much as ruler. 
	- For example, the "fiction" of divine right made everyone a subject of a faultless king, but also created a standard of conduct to which parliament could hold the king accountable.
	- Another phenomenon is that a fiction taken to literally by a constituency - often one it was intended to help woo to the side of the government - may destroy the very government it was at one point intended to support.
- It is when these fictions interact with the facts of our world that strange things start to happen. 
	- For example, fictions may collapse when they fail to resemble the facts of the world closely enough.
	- Likewise, when the facts of the world stray too far from the fictions we believe in, we tend to engage in reformation or revolution to bring those facts back in line with those fictions. 
- Ultimately, fictions that support authority tend also - in "devious" ways - to be those that support liberty.
