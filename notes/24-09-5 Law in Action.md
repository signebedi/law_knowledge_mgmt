#### Legal _Fictions_

I asked you to complete this rather short reading because I think it frames the broader course conversation very effectively. What is the point of government and the law? What is Edmund Morgan trying to say about the relationship between the government and the governed? How about opinion and force? Is force always on the side on the governed?


What do we think Morgan means by a "Fiction"?  
- The use of language here is important. In this course, I use the term "story" ... but hopefully it conveys the same idea without the negative connotation. Other language includes "narrative" and the even-more cynical "lie".

What do we think about what Morgan has to say about reform and revolution?  
- Viz. that they occur when fiction strays too far from reality, and there is a social force to bring reality more in-line with fiction, or fiction more in line with reality. 

What do we think about what Morgan has to say about liberty?  
- Viz. that liberty may depend on the very fictions that were intended to support authority.  
- This invites an interesting discussion about liberty vs. authority; justice vs. stability. It may also be worth considering some of the ideas discussed in the introduction to Starr's _Social Transformation of American Medicine_.

How ought we tie this back into the broader course? Are there fictions that exist in American law today? What are they? How should we approach them? Is Morgan's approach to this phenomenon particularly useful?
